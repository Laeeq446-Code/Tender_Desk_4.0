Methodology

How Tender Desk decides what you see.

1. Collection

Adapters scan public procurement portals four times daily (09:00, 12:00, 15:00, 18:00 PKT): PPRA EPMS and EPADS at federal level, provincial PPRAs, and multilateral sources (World Bank, ADB, UNGM). Each source is isolated: if one portal changes or fails, the header pill turns red rather than the feed silently going stale. The header shows the last completed scan.

2. Deduplication and change tracking

The same tender often appears on multiple portals under different reference numbers. Records are matched on a normalised title, buyer and closing-date fingerprint; duplicates are folded into one row with the alternates listed. Corrigenda matter: changes to closing dates or status are logged and shown on the tender's detail view.

3. Classification: can Jazz deliver this?

The question asked is not "is this digital?" but "can Jazz deliver this?". Every tender is read from its title and description text (portal categories are ignored as unreliable) and sorted into a lane. Core: Jazz can bid largely alone; connectivity, enterprise mobility, IoT and M2M, messaging and contact centre, digital financial services, cloud and hosting. Partner-led: a consortium is needed; managed security, smart city, systems integration, data and AI. Signal: not biddable, but evidence that an institution has digitisation budget; laptops, licences, IT services. Everything else, civil works, power grid equipment, vehicles, is excluded. Each tender stores the exact keywords that produced its tag, shown on its detail page, so every decision is auditable.

3b. The Other category

PPRA covers public-sector procurement only. Universities, private banks and large corporates buy the same connectivity, cloud and security capability but publish on their own websites. The Other source scans a curated roster of those buyers: Islamabad and Rawalpindi universities (NUST, QAU, COMSATS, IIUI, Air, Bahria, NUML, PIEAS, Arid, AIOU), private banks (HBL, UBL, Bank Alfalah, Meezan, Faysal, Askari, Bank of Punjab) and corporates including PTCL, K-Electric and PSX. This is a fixed roster rather than an open web crawl: search engines block automated querying within a few requests and return mostly noise, whereas a known roster is precise, repeatable and legally clean. Filter to it by selecting Other under Source.

3c. Reconciliation and the miss report

The Reconcile tab runs the check in both directions. Forward: each row of an uploaded list is matched against everything collected, returning found, possible or missing with a confidence score. Inverse: every biddable tender the portals published inside the same date window that does not appear on the uploaded list is surfaced under Missed by us, ranked by fit score and downloadable as CSV. Matching rests on shared information rather than similarity. Two tenders sharing the phrase procurement of IT equipment are highly similar and share almost no information, because those words appear in hundreds of notices; two sharing digital supply chain financing share a great deal. A match therefore requires distinctive shared vocabulary, measured against the corpus itself, and the portal title must carry enough content to be evidence at all. Your description is compared against both our title and our stored description. Buyer identity corroborates. The closing date is a tiebreaker only, since agencies cluster bid openings on common days and internal lists record internal deadlines. Comparison is stemmed and spelling tolerant, so digitisation, digitization and digitalization are one term. The forward pass audits the list. The inverse pass audits coverage, and is where opportunities nobody noticed internally appear.

3d. The brief

The landing tab answers a different question from the feed. The feed asks what exists; the brief asks what changed and what needs a decision now. It carries three fixed sections: biddable tenders first seen in the last two days, biddable tenders closing within seven, and corrigenda that moved a date or status on a tracked tender. Core and Partner-led lanes only, capped at ten rows per section. An empty section is a valid answer and states so explicitly, so silence is never ambiguous.

3e. Intelligence from awards

A tender notice describes what is happening now. A contract award describes who holds an account, at what price, against how many bidders, and for how long. PPRA publishes awards, so the contract term stated in the wording, typically "for a period of three (03) years", yields a re-tender date years ahead of the notice. The Intelligence tab turns that into a renewal pipeline, an incumbency map, price bands per product line, and a list of single-bid awards where an incumbent went unchallenged. This is the layer a tender subscription cannot supply, because it is analysis of procurement behaviour rather than distribution of notices.

3f. Newspaper ingestion

Commercial aggregators differentiate on newspaper coverage rather than portals, and their operation is largely manual: staff read the papers and key the advertisements in. The Newspapers tab automates the English-language half of that. A PDF carrying a text layer is read directly. A scanned page is rasterised and passed through OCR, then segmented into advertisements, filtered to those carrying a procurement heading, and parsed for buyer, subject, reference, closing date and contact. Urdu is deliberately excluded: recognition quality is poor enough that a wrong closing date would enter the database, which is worse than a missing tender. Every saved row is flagged for review.

4. Scoring

The fit score (0-100) reflects lane, keyword density, and whether the buyer is a named key account (NTC, USF, NADRA, PITB, FBR and others). It orders the queue; it does not replace judgement.

4b. Nothing is discarded

Every row a portal returns is stored. Cross-source duplicates are tagged rather than dropped, so the Everything scope shows the complete raw harvest including duplicates and rows classified as irrelevant. The curated scopes (Bids, +Signal) hide duplicates for readability. If a tender is missing from Everything, it was absent from the portal at scan time rather than filtered out here.

5. Known limits

Portals serve a rolling window, not a full archive, so a tender floated and closed before scanning began cannot be recovered. History begins when scanning began; portals only serve a rolling window, so depth compounds daily. Classification is keyword-based and tuned over time, false positives are expected and correctable. Values are as published; many portals do not disclose them.
